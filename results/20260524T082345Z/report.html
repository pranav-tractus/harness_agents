Sec. 06

What we recommend doing next

A short list of decisions this run unlocks — written for stakeholders, not just engineers.

01
Ship Sonnet 4-6 as the default in production
At 98.6% accuracy and ~13 seconds per document, it is the highest-quality option we tested. Any workload where being right matters more than being fast should be running on this today.
02
Use OpenAI 5.4 for the high-volume tier
For customers or pipelines where we are processing tens of thousands of documents and need sub-10-second response, OpenAI 5.4 delivers 92.7% accuracy in 7 seconds — a strong cost/throughput story.
03
Retire Gemini 2.5 Pro from the lineup
It is the slowest model and the least accurate. There is no scenario in this run where Gemini 2.5 Pro is the right answer. Removing it simplifies our offering and our costs.
04
Keep investing in the validation layer
It delivered a 3.5-point accuracy gain on its first version and only regressed 9% of runs. The next iteration could plausibly close another 2–3 points of the remaining gap to 100%.
05
Price the offering with confidence
We now have hard numbers on accuracy, latency, and consistency across customer datasets. That is enough to build tiered SLAs and pricing — quality tier on Sonnet, throughput tier on OpenAI 5.4.
06
Pilot worked-example prompting next
All of these results are zero-shot. Adding a handful of curated examples to the prompt is a low-cost experiment that has historically moved accuracy 2–5 points further. Worth a single sprint to verify.
